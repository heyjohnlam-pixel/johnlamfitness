What's working
✦

Genuine recomposition — fat down, muscle up simultaneously

Lost 3.8 lb of fat while gaining 3.5 lb of lean mass between January and June DEXA scans. Body recomposition in a caloric deficit — the ideal outcome of consistent heavy strength training.

↑

Protein almost nailed — 97% of target on logged days

Averaging 140.8g against a 145g target. Protein is the most important macro for muscle retention in a deficit and you're effectively hitting it. This is a real strength in the data.

↑

VO₂ max climbing — 41.5 → 44.7 mL/kg/min

Meaningful cardiovascular improvement over 6 weeks of tracking despite the caloric deficit. Aerobic fitness is genuinely trending up.

→

Steps consistently above target — averaging 13,200/day

Exceeding the 10k daily step target adds significant NEAT and compounds total energy expenditure without adding training stress.

Watchout areas
!

Extreme calorie variability — std dev ±663 kcal

On logged days, calories ranged from 100 to 3,161 kcal. A standard deviation of 663 is enormous — some days are deep under target (799 kcal) while others are significantly over (3,161 kcal). The average of 1,518 looks okay but masks this instability. Consistency beats average.

↑

VAT increased 81% — from 1.28 to 2.32 lb

Visceral fat rose while all other fat metrics declined. Most likely cause: the deep deficit combined with high training load elevating cortisol chronically. Eating closer to target (1,500–1,675 kcal) consistently is the primary lever. A/G ratio of 1.32 should be below 1.0.

◯

Carbs chronically under-hit — only 27% of days at target

Only 11 of 41 logged days hit ≥150g carbs. Averaging 121g against a 150g target. This is the macro most connected to training performance, energy, and preventing the cortisol response that's likely driving VAT up.

◯

HIIT nearly absent — only 1 session in 3 months

Prescribed is 2×/week of Tabata (4 min each, 160+ BPM). Despite the short duration, these are all-out efforts and specifically target visceral fat through improved insulin sensitivity — especially relevant given the VAT finding.

~

HRV high variance signals inconsistent recovery

Range of 23–69 ms across 30 days. Low-HRV clusters (23–34 ms) appear around high-output weeks, suggesting training load isn't being fully absorbed. Protecting sleep and eating consistently on rest days would smooth this out.

Top 3 actions
1

Reduce day-to-day calorie swings — consistency over average

Your average (1,518 kcal) is actually close to target — the problem is the swing. Aim for 1,400–1,675 every day rather than 800 one day and 3,000 the next. Pre-logging dinner in the morning is the single most effective tool for this.

2

Prioritize carbs on training days — target ≥150g

You're hitting carb target only 27% of days. On the 3 strength days each week, hit 150g carbs before anything else. Add 40g cooked rice to lunch and dinner — that alone closes most of the gap most days.

3

Add one HIIT session per week

Tabata-style, 20 min minimum, 160+ BPM. Missing piece for metabolic rate and visceral fat mobilization. You have the capacity — VO₂ max is strong and trending up.

Key nutrition rules
1
Pre-log dinner every morning. Open Cronometer, log your planned dinner first. This anchors the day and prevents the 3,000 kcal blowouts in your data.
2
Same macros every day — no cycling. 1,675 kcal · 150g carbs · 145g protein · 55g fat regardless of training type. Consistency is the goal, not periodization.
3
200g cooked rice = ~45g carbs. Add one extra rice portion at lunch or dinner on training days. This single swap closes the carb gap without changing anything else.
4
Stay 1,400–1,750 every day. Your avg is 1,518 — close. The problem is the swing (100 to 3,161 kcal). Consistency beats perfection. 1,500 every day beats 800 + 3,000 alternating.
5
Creatine post-workout, every session. Doesn't require timing precision — just daily. 5g creatine monohydrate. Already in your protocol, don't skip it.
